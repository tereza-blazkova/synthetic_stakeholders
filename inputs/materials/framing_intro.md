# Welcome

Welcome, and thank you for taking part in this workshop. Today you are here as a
student or as a student advisor at this university. The workshop is in English
and lasts about three hours.

## Why we are here

The overall aim is to **reach out to students who are experiencing academic
difficulties**, so that support can be offered early.

- This is relevant for all students: a model like this could become relevant for
  anyone, including you.
- Please also think beyond yourself, from within your role: your peers, your
  fellow students, your colleagues, and the students you advise.

Today the university already contacts students who fall behind: a student is
identified when they have passed fewer course credits than expected. [ADDED: the
current credit-based rule, described at the level of the survey's "very high
explainability" example.] A predictive model based on data could be a way to
improve this current system. But using one raises many questions and trade-offs
about what to prefer, mostly:

- **privacy versus accuracy**: which data the model may use, and
- **explainability versus accuracy**: how well a decision must be explainable.

## The goal of the workshop

The goal is to explore **under what conditions, if any, such a model would be
acceptable**, and how those conditions could be reflected in the design of the
system. There are no right or wrong answers.
